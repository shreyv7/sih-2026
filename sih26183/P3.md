# SIH26183 — ROLE 3 RESEARCH REPORT
## Entity Attribution, Exchange Wallet Architecture & Evidence Chains
### Problem Statement: Real-Time Identification of Fraud-Linked Cryptocurrency Exchanges from Victim-Reported Suspect Wallet Addresses through Automated Blockchain Analytics
**Domain:** Blockchain & Cybersecurity | **Target Ministry:** Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) / Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) | **Category:** Software

## 1. Executive Summary & Role 3 Mission

- **The Core Attribution Challenge:** An on-chain cryptocurrency address is merely a pseudonymous alphanumeric string (e.g., `0x28C6...` on EVM or `TNDpYv...` on TRON). It does not inherently reveal an individual's name, physical address, corporate entity, or legal jurisdiction. Role 3 is responsible for answering the critical investigative question: **"Whose wallet is this, which exchange controls it, and what is the defensible evidentiary basis for that conclusion?"**
- **The Glass-Box Mandate vs. Proprietary Black Boxes:** Commercial intelligence platforms (Chainalysis, TRM Labs) output proprietary, unexplained entity tags and risk ratings. Under Indian criminal jurisprudence, such outputs are legally fragile. Role 3 implements a **Glass-Box Attribution Architecture** that exposes every link in the evidentiary chain: source provenance, transaction hashes, sweep heuristics, corroborating OSINT, and explicit confidence scores.
- **The "Ownership vs. Association" Separation:** Interacting with an exchange does not mean the exchange owns the suspect address, nor does it imply the exchange participated in the fraud. The system enforces strict separation between:
  1. *On-chain transaction association* (value transfer).
  2. *Custodial infrastructure role* (personal deposit address vs. omnibus hot wallet).
  3. *Beneficial account ownership* (the individual customer who completed KYC).
  4. *Criminal culpability* (the fraud perpetrator).
- **The Central Discovery (CEX Deposit Sweep Mechanics):** Fraudsters never deposit stolen funds directly into an exchange's public hot wallet. They deposit into an unlabelled, temporary **personal deposit address**. The key to unmasking the destination exchange lies in detecting the **automated secondary sweep** wherein the exchange pulls funds from the deposit address into its publicly verified omnibus hot wallet, often accompanied by native gas subsidization.
- **Hackathon Prototype Scope:** Building a local, pre-indexed SQLite/PostgreSQL database of verified hot wallets and deposit patterns for the top exchanges active in India (Binance, Bybit, KuCoin, OKX, WazirX, CoinDCX, CoinSwitch) on **TRON (TRC-20 USDT)** and **Ethereum/Polygon (ERC-20 USDT)**, backed by public Proof-of-Reserves disclosures and explorer tags.

## 2. Technical Foundations & Core Terminology

| Concept / Term | Technical Definition | Operational Relevance to SIH26183 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Address** | A 20-byte (EVM) or 21-byte (TRON) public key hash representing an account on the distributed ledger. | The raw, unlabelled starting node $V_0$ in an investigation. |
| **Wallet** | The software, hardware, or operational key-management interface controlling one or more addresses. | An operational boundary. One wallet may manage dozens of derived addresses (HD wallets). |
| **Entity** | A real-world organization, corporation, service, or individual (e.g., Binance, Uniswap, Tornado Cash). | The ultimate real-world attribution target. |
| **Beneficial User** | The individual human account holder who operates an account within a centralized exchange. | The actual suspect holding KYC credentials on file with the exchange. |
| **Address Label** | External metadata associating an on-chain address with an entity, category, or risk tag. | Metadata, **not** an intrinsic on-chain fact. Must include provenance, source, and date. |
| **Glass-Box Attribution** | An attribution methodology where every conclusion is accompanied by auditable, inspectable evidence. | Essential for legal admissibility under **Section 63 of Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023**. |
| **Proof of Reserves (PoR)** | Public cryptographic disclosures by exchanges demonstrating on-chain custody of client assets. | Gold-standard, first-party ground-truth dataset for verifying official exchange hot and cold wallets. |

### 5.1 The 4-Stage Deposit Lifecycle
1. **Stage 1 (Address Generation):** When an exchange user navigates to "Deposit USDT", the CEX server generates a unique, counterfactual deposit address mapped internally to that user's Account ID (UID). This address is dormant and unlabelled on block explorers.
2. **Stage 2 (Fund Inflow):** The scammer transfers stolen funds into this deposit address. At this moment, public block explorers display only an incoming transfer to an anonymous hex string.
3. **Stage 3 (Gas Parent Subsidization):** On account-based chains (EVM / TRON), moving tokens requires native gas (ETH/TRX). Because fresh deposit addresses contain zero native gas, the exchange's automated backend sends a nominal transaction (e.g., 0.002 ETH or 15 TRX) from a central operational wallet to the deposit address.
4. **Stage 4 (Automated Consolidation Sweep):** Within minutes or hours ($\Delta t \le 24 \text{ hours}$), an automated sweeper bot executes a transaction pulling the entire token balance into the exchange's main omnibus hot wallet.

### 5.2 Critical Forensic Limitation: Internal Ledger Invisibility
Once stolen funds enter an omnibus hot wallet, they are instantly co-mingled with funds from millions of legitimate users. **Further on-chain tracing is technically impossible and forensically invalid.** An investigator cannot trace "which specific hot wallet withdrawal corresponds to the victim's funds" because withdrawals are fulfilled from the exchange's pooled liquidity.

> **Operational Mandate:** Tracing must terminate at the CEX omnibus hot wallet. The investigation must pivot immediately from on-chain graph traversal to off-chain legal requisition under **Section 94 BNSS** targeting the specific intermediate deposit address identified in Stage 2.

### Core Anti-Overclaiming Rules
1. **Never conflate the platform with the perpetrator:** An exchange is a financial utility (VASP). A scammer depositing funds into an exchange does not make the exchange a criminal syndicate.
2. **Distinguish custodial control from account operation:** The exchange controls the cryptographic private keys; the criminal user controls the off-chain trading account.
3. **Acknowledge "Unknown" without penalty:** If an address forwards funds to an unlabelled wallet that never sweeps into a known service, the system must classify the destination as **"Unknown / Unattributed Private Wallet"** rather than forcing a low-confidence guess.

## 7. Deterministic & Rule-Based Confidence Scoring Engine

To eliminate proprietary "black-box" risk scores, Role 3 defines a transparent, additive mathematical confidence framework:

### 7.1 The Additive Confidence Formula
$$\text{Confidence Score } (S) = \min\left(100, \sum_{i=1}^{n} W_i \cdot E_i\right) - P_{\text{contradiction}}$$

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                 Evidence Weighting Matrix (Weights W_i)                                │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┬─────────────┬───────────────────┤
│ Evidence Signal (E_i)                                                │ Weight (W_i)│ Tier Alignment    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────┼───────────────────┤
│ Direct match against First-Party Proof-of-Reserves Registry           │ +50 pts     │ Tier 1 (Direct)   │
│ Direct match against Verified Explorer Name Tag (Etherscan/TronScan) │ +40 pts     │ Tier 2 (Verified) │
│ Automated sweep into verified CEX Hot Wallet ($\le 24$h, $v \approx v$)│ +35 pts     │ Tier 3 (Behavior) │
│ Native gas subsidization from known CEX Sweeper / Operational Bot    │ +20 pts     │ Tier 3 (Behavior) │
│ Corroborated malicious reports on Chainabuse / CryptoScamDB ($\ge 3$)│ +15 pts     │ Tier 4 (OSINT)    │
│ Verified ENS domain or Web3 domain mapping to official entity        │ +10 pts     │ Tier 4 (OSINT)    │
│ Statistical heuristic clustering (CIOH / multi-hop co-spending)      │ +5 pts      │ Tier 5 (Heuristic)│
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────┴───────────────────┤
│ Contradiction Penalties (P_contradiction):                                                             │
│ • Target address has active outgoing transfers to other competing exchanges (-40 pts)                 │
│ • Address is a verified smart contract or liquidity pool (-50 pts)                                     │
│ • Sweep delay exceeds 7 days without CEX gas subsidization (-20 pts)                                  │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 7.2 Confidence Level Thresholds
- **Confirmed (Score $\ge 90$):** First-party cryptographic disclosure or verified explorer hot wallet match. Grounds for immediate legal freeze.
- **High Confidence Association (Score $75–89$):** Documented sweep into a verified hot wallet backed by gas subsidization. Clear attribution as a custodial deposit address.
- **Probable Association (Score $50–74$):** Sweep observed without gas corroboration, or multiple corroborating OSINT threat reports.
- **Tentative / Low Confidence (Score $25–49$):** Pure heuristic co-spending or uncorroborated single-source report.
- **Unknown (Score $< 25$):** Insufficient data. Explicitly preserved to prevent false-positive misdirection.

## 11. Strategic Evaluator Q&A & Defensibility Guide

### Q1: If an address interacts with Binance, why can't we say "Binance owns this address"?
**Answer:** Because that conflates the *custodial infrastructure provider* with the *beneficial user*. Binance owns the server infrastructure and private key generation script, but the account is operated by an individual user who deposited funds. Calling it "Binance's wallet" implies the exchange is the fraudster, which is legally erroneous and inadmissible in court.

### Q2: What happens if an exchange changes its hot wallet addresses?
**Answer:** Centralized exchanges periodically rotate hot wallets for operational security. Our system addresses this through:
1. Automated periodic syncing with published Proof-of-Reserves snapshots.
2. Explorer verification clustering (monitoring high-volume aggregation hubs with $> 50,000$ daily transactions).
3. Historical timestamp scoping (matching the sweep against the hot wallet registry active at the time of the transaction block height).

### Q3: How do we handle unlabelled addresses that never sweep their funds?
**Answer:** The system classifies them as **"Self-Custody Private Wallet"** with attribution confidence **"Unknown."** We never force a probabilistic guess without concrete sweep or registry corroboration. Preserving "Unknown" protects the investigating officer from pursuing false leads.

### Q4: Why is an on-chain trace alone insufficient to convict a scammer?
**Answer:** Blockchains prove *what* happened between addresses, not *who* executed it. The on-chain trace provides the necessary probable cause to compel the custodial exchange under **Section 94 BNSS** to unmask the real-world identity (KYC, bank account, IP) of the account holder.
