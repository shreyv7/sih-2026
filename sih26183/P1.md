# SIH26183 — ROLE 1 RESEARCH REPORT
## Existing Market Solutions & Competitive Analysis
### Problem Statement: Real-Time Identification of Fraud-Linked Cryptocurrency Exchanges from Victim-Reported Suspect Wallet Addresses through Automated Blockchain Analytics
**Domain:** Blockchain & Cybersecurity | **Target Ministry:** Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) / Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) | **Category:** Software

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Commercial Market Duopoly & Walled Gardens:** The blockchain intelligence sector is dominated by enterprise vendors—primarily Chainalysis, TRM Labs, Elliptic, and Crystal Intelligence. These platforms rely on proprietary, closed-source clustering algorithms and privately curated address-attribution databases that are completely inaccessible to the public.
- **Severe Pricing & Procurement Barriers:** Commercial forensic suites cost between $15,000 and $50,000+ annually per seat. They require institutional vetting and lengthy procurement cycles, effectively locking out local police station cyber cells, state-level investigating officers (IOs), academic researchers, and student developers.
- **The "Audit vs. Triage" Architectural Disconnect:** Existing enterprise tools are engineered for multi-month forensic accounting, tax compliance, and institutional AML/CFT risk management. They are **not** optimized for rapid first-responder triage—the critical "golden hour" immediately following a cyber complaint when an investigator must identify the destination centralized exchange (CEX) and freeze suspect accounts before funds are off-ramped into fiat currency.
- **Deficiencies of Free & Crowdsourced Tools:** Free block explorers (e.g., Etherscan, Tronscan, Blockchair) provide raw, unlinked transaction dumps requiring tedious manual tracking across dozens of browser tabs. Crowdsourced platforms (e.g., Arkham Intelligence, Breadcrumbs) provide valuable label data but lack automated, multi-hop forward-tracing heuristics capable of detecting custodial exchange sweep architectures.
- **The Core SIH Opportunity:** A lightweight, automated triage engine tailored for Indian Law Enforcement Agencies (LEAs). The system takes a victim-reported suspect address, algorithmically traces outgoing fund flows across 1–4 hops, unmasks custodial CEX deposit addresses via deterministic sweep heuristics, and instantly auto-populates statutory freeze requisitions under **Section 94 BNSS** (formerly Section 91 CrPC) addressed directly to the compliance desks of FIU-IND registered exchanges.

## 2. Technical Foundations & Core Terminology

To evaluate existing tools and design an effective triage solution, the following technical concepts must be clearly distinguished:

- **Public Address vs. Off-Chain Identity:** An address is an alphanumeric public key hash (e.g., `0x71C...` on EVM chains, `TYD...` on TRON, `1A1z...` on Bitcoin). Blockchain ledgers record state transitions and value transfers between addresses with mathematical finality, but contain zero native identity data (no legal names, phone numbers, or IP addresses). Real-world identity resides strictly on the off-chain servers of regulated entities (exchanges) that enforce Know-Your-Customer (KYC) compliance.
- **Account-Based Model (EVM / TRON) vs. UTXO Model (Bitcoin):**
  - *Account-Based (Ethereum, Polygon, BNB Chain, TRON):* Operates like a banking ledger. Transactions directly debit the sender's balance and credit the recipient's balance. Token transfers (e.g., USDT via ERC-20 or TRC-20) are executed by calling a smart contract function (`transfer(address to, uint256 value)`), emitting on-chain event logs rather than native value transfers. Over 90% of cyber fraud complaints in India involve USDT on TRC-20 or EVM chains.
  - *UTXO (Bitcoin, Litecoin):* Operates with unspent transaction outputs. Transactions consume existing outputs and generate new outputs, frequently creating "change addresses." Tracing relies on heuristics like Common-Input-Ownership (CIOH) to cluster wallets.
- **CEX Wallet Architecture (Omnibus Hot Wallets vs. Cold Vaults vs. Deposit Addresses):**
  - *Hot Wallet:* An internet-connected, automated wallet used by an exchange to process live user withdrawals and liquidity transfers. These addresses have massive transaction volumes (hundreds of thousands of transactions daily) and are publicly recognized by block explorers.
  - *Cold Vault:* An offline, multi-signature or MPC-governed hardware storage vault holding the exchange's long-term surplus reserves.
  - *Personal Custodial Deposit Address:* A unique, counterfactual address generated by the exchange for an individual customer's account. When funds arrive at this address, the user's off-chain internal account balance is credited. Critically, **these addresses are initially unlabelled on public explorers**.
- **The Automated Deposit Sweep Mechanism:** A centralized exchange does not store customer funds permanently in individual deposit addresses. Instead, automated sweeper bots periodically execute a "sweep" transaction, transferring the aggregated funds from the deposit address into the exchange's omnibus hot wallet. This sweep is the definitive on-chain signature connecting an unknown deposit address to a specific exchange.
- **Multi-Hop Layering & Obfuscation:** Laundering tactics employed by fraudsters to break the immediate link between the scam collection wallet and the cash-out point. Common typologies include:
  - *Rapid Forwarding:* Moving the entire balance through 2 to 4 intermediary "burner" wallets within minutes.
  - *Peeling Chains:* Iteratively sending a small portion to a burner/cash-out address while forwarding the larger residual balance to a fresh change address.
  - *Split and Merge:* Splitting funds into smaller chunks across multiple parallel wallets, then recombining them at a single deposit gateway.
- **Graph Modeling & Algorithmic Traversal:** Formulating the blockchain ledger as a Directed Graph $G = (V, E)$, where vertices $V$ represent blockchain addresses and directed edges $E$ represent transactions with attributes: timestamp $t$, asset type $a$, value $v$, and transaction hash $h$. Graph algorithms (e.g., Breadth-First Search with value/time pruning) allow automated money tracing without manual inspection.

## 5. Detailed Gap Analysis (Why Current Tools Fail Frontline Responders)

1. **Extreme Financial Exclusion ($15,000–$50,000+ per year):**
   - Commercial forensic platforms price their products exclusively for national intelligence agencies, federal law enforcement (e.g., CBI, ED), and top tier cryptocurrency compliance teams.
   - Ground-level investigating officers in district cyber police stations and state cyber cells are left entirely without tooling, forcing them to rely on free block explorers that lack graph intelligence.
2. **The "Golden Hour" Latency & Manual Expansion Fatigue:**
   - Scammers rapidly liquidate stolen funds via P2P desks on centralized exchanges within **30 to 120 minutes** of receiving victim deposits.
   - Existing enterprise tools require the investigator to manually click, expand, and evaluate transaction branches on a visual canvas. When an address splits funds across 10 or 20 branches, the investigator must manually inspect each path. This manual process causes investigation times to exceed 45 minutes, forfeiting the opportunity to freeze funds before fiat conversion.
3. **The Unlabelled Custodial Deposit Address Blind Spot:**
   - Public block explorers label only well-known, central exchange infrastructure (e.g., "Binance: Hot Wallet 14", "WazirX: Hot Wallet 2").
   - Fraudsters never deposit stolen crypto directly into a public hot wallet; they deposit into a uniquely generated, temporary **personal deposit address**.
   - Because public explorers do not tag personal deposit addresses, an investigator inspecting an explorer tab sees an unknown, unlabelled address (`0x9B4...`) and cannot determine which exchange controls it unless they proactively trace the subsequent internal sweep transaction.
4. **Opaque "Black-Box" ML Risk Scores vs. Evidentiary Admissibility:**
   - Commercial AML platforms provide proprietary risk scores (e.g., "Risk Rating: 8.7 / 10 - High Risk").
   - Under the Indian legal system (Indian Evidence Act, 1872 / Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023), proprietary algorithmic scores that cannot be independently audited or mathematically reconstructed are vulnerable to challenge in court.
   - Frontline cyber cells require **transparent, deterministic evidence chains** (exact transaction hashes, block timestamps, transfer values, and documented sweep transactions) rather than opaque probabilistic risk scores.
5. **Absence of a Statutory Legal Requisition Bridge:**
   - None of the commercial or free tools integrate with local statutory legal frameworks.
   - After identifying an exchange, the investigator must manually locate the exchange's legal nodal contact details, draft a formal requisition letter under **Section 94 BNSS** (or Section 91 CrPC), copy and paste wallet addresses and transaction hashes, and format the document. This manual administrative step adds 30 to 60 minutes of critical delay.

### 6.1 Targeted High-Velocity Chains
- Rather than attempting to support 50+ blockchains, the prototype must focus on:
  - **TRON (TRC-20 USDT):** Accounts for over 70% of retail cyber scam fund flows in Southeast Asia and India due to minimal transaction fees.
  - **Ethereum / Polygon / BNB Chain (ERC-20 USDT/USDC):** The primary EVM platforms used for crypto laundering and decentralized exchange (DEX) swaps.

### 6.2 Resilient Ingestion Layer & Caching Architecture
- **API Aggregation:** Ingest raw transaction data using free-tier developer APIs:
  - Etherscan API (5 calls/sec on free tier).
  - TronGrid API (REST endpoints for TRC-20 transfers).
  - Alchemy / Infura (for EVM event logs).
- **Persistent Key-Value Cache:** To operate within free-tier API rate limits and prevent repeated network requests, all queried address transaction lists and account metadata are cached locally in SQLite or Redis with a 24-hour TTL (Time-to-Live).

### 6.3 Algorithmic Forward-Tracing Pipeline (Pruned BFS)
- **Algorithm:** Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) extraction via Breadth-First Search (BFS) starting at root suspect address $V_0$.
- **Depth Limit ($k \le 4$):** Over 92% of fraud laundering flows reach a custodial cash-out exchange within 1 to 4 hops.
- **Value-Taint Pruning:** Micro-dust spam transactions ($< \$50$ USDT or $< 5\%$ of the initial victim loss) are automatically pruned from the search tree to eliminate graph explosion.
- **Time-Window Filtering:** Only transactions occurring **after** the incident timestamp reported in the victim's complaint are evaluated, ignoring irrelevant historical activity.

### 6.4 Deterministic CEX Sweep Attribution Heuristic
- The system maintains a curated, verified registry of hot wallet addresses for major exchanges operating in India (Binance, Bybit, KuCoin, OKX, WazirX, CoinDCX, CoinSwitch).
- **Attribution Logic:**
  $$\text{If } (A \xrightarrow{\text{transfer } (v)} B) \text{ and } (B \xrightarrow{\text{sweep } (v - \text{fee})} \text{CEX\_Hot\_Wallet}) \implies B \text{ is attributed as a } \mathbf{CEX\_Deposit\_Address}$$
- **Gas Parent Heuristic (EVM & TRON):** Many exchanges pre-fund new deposit accounts with tiny amounts of native gas (e.g., 0.002 ETH or 15 TRX) from a central operational wallet immediately before executing the token sweep. Correlating the source of gas funding reinforces deposit address attribution.

### 6.5 Statutory Dossier & Requisition Automation
- Integrates a document generation engine (e.g., Python `ReportLab` or Node.js `Puppeteer`) to output a ready-to-issue legal notice:
  - Formatted under **Section 94 BNSS** (Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023) / **Section 91 CrPC**.
  - Includes a digital evidence certificate under **Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act** / **Section 63 of Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023**.
  - Pre-populated with the target exchange's designated Grievance & Nodal Officer email addresses (registered with FIU-IND).

## 7. Explicit Technical Boundaries (What NOT to Attempt)

Maintaining technical credibility during the SIH evaluation requires clear boundaries distinguishing realistic engineering from unachievable claims:

1. **Direct On-Chain PII Extraction is Impossible:**
   - Blockchains do not record real-world identities. The system must **never** claim to "identify the individual criminal."
   - The system's true objective is to **identify the regulated custodial entity (exchange)** holding the scammer's KYC records, enabling law enforcement to serve targeted legal notices.
2. **De-Anonymizing Zero-Knowledge Mixers is Infeasible:**
   - Standard graph traversal algorithms cannot deterministically trace funds through decentralized zero-knowledge privacy pools (e.g., Tornado Cash) or privacy coins (e.g., Monero RingCT).
   - If funds enter a mixer, the system must terminate the forward trace at the mixer contract, flag the interaction as an illicit obfuscation event, and document the mixer contract address as an evidence milestone.
3. **Self-Hosted Full Archive Nodes are Unnecessary:**
   - Operating a private Ethereum full archive node requires 12+ terabytes of high-performance NVMe storage, dedicated high-speed bandwidth, and days of initial syncing.
   - For a hackathon MVP, relying on reliable public RPC providers and developer APIs with an intelligent caching layer is the correct architectural choice.
4. **Universal Multi-Chain Indexing is Impractical:**
   - Attempting to support 100+ chains simultaneously results in fragile, shallow integrations. Prioritizing EVM and TRON delivers over 90% real-world operational coverage.

### Module Breakdown:
- **Module 1: Automated Forward-Tracing Crawler Engine**
  - Accepts suspect address, blockchain, token contract (e.g., USDT), and incident timestamp.
  - Queries transaction history, discards incoming transfers and pre-incident transactions, and recursively iterates through recipients up to depth $k = 4$.
- **Module 2: CEX Sweep & Deposit Detector**
  - Compares every downstream recipient against a local SQLite database of known exchange hot wallets.
  - Detects automated sweep transactions occurring within a configurable time delta ($\Delta t \le 24 \text{ hours}$) where $v_{\text{sweep}} \approx v_{\text{deposit}}$.
- **Module 3: Interactive Forensic Visualizer**
  - Renders a clean, color-coded node-link graph:
    - *Blue Nodes:* Victim and intermediate burner wallets.
    - *Red Nodes:* Identified suspect wallets.
    - *Orange Nodes:* Identified personal CEX deposit addresses.
    - *Green Nodes:* Verified exchange hot wallets (destination endpoint).
  - Clicking any edge reveals the exact transaction hash, timestamp, transferred value, and gas fee.
- **Module 4: Deterministic Risk Profiler**
  - Calculates transparent behavioral metrics:
    - *Velocity:* Time elapsed between fund receipt and forwarding ($< 15\text{ mins} \implies \text{Automated Layering}$).
    - *Peeling Ratio:* Fraction of funds retained vs. forwarded.
    - *Fan-out Degree:* Number of outgoing recipient addresses spawned in a single block.
- **Module 5: Statutory Dossier & Legal Freeze Generator**
  - Auto-fills a police requisition template:
    - Target Exchange Legal Compliance Desk.
    - Suspect Wallet Address & Target Deposit Address.
    - Exact Transaction Hashes and Timestamps (UTC and IST).
    - Specific Legal Freeze Directive under Section 94 BNSS / Section 91 CrPC.

### Analysis of Open-Source Codebases & Technical Precedents

To ensure rapid, robust prototype development, our architecture leverages proven open-source paradigms:

1. **s0md3v/Orbit (`github.com/s0md3v/Orbit`):**
   - *Design Paradigm:* Demonstrates that recursive multi-hop blockchain crawling can be executed efficiently via Python using standard REST APIs without running local blockchain nodes.
   - *Adoption in SIH:* We adopt Orbit's clean API abstraction and queue-based traversal model, extending it to parse ERC-20/TRC-20 smart contract token transfers and detect sweep mechanics.
2. **mattconsto/blockchain-tracer (`github.com/mattconsto/blockchain-tracer`):**
   - *Design Paradigm:* Implements mathematical taint-tracking models (Proportional Haircut vs. First-In-First-Out) on transaction graphs.
   - *Adoption in SIH:* We adapt the value-taint thresholding logic to eliminate micro-dust transactions and focus exclusively on dominant fund flows.
3. **graphsense/graphsense-lib (`github.com/graphsense/graphsense-lib`):**
   - *Design Paradigm:* Academic-grade library for address clustering, entity resolution, and forensic ledger serialization.
   - *Adoption in SIH:* We use GraphSense's entity modeling conventions to separate observed on-chain transactions from derived attribution inferences.

### Statutory & Regulatory Authorities (India)
1. **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023:**
   - *Section 94 (formerly Section 91 CrPC):* Summons to produce document or other thing (statutory legal basis for issuing freeze requisitions to cryptocurrency exchanges).
2. **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023:**
   - *Section 63 (formerly Section 65B Indian Evidence Act, 1872):* Admissibility of electronic records and requirements for digital forensic certification.
3. **Financial Intelligence Unit - India (FIU-IND):**
   - *AML & CFT Guidelines for Virtual Digital Asset Service Providers (VDA SPs):* Mandates that all registered crypto exchanges (domestic and international serving Indian users) must register as Reporting Entities under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002, and maintain designated Nodal Officers to comply with LEA directives.
4. **Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) & I4C:**
   - *National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (NCRP) & Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System (CFCFRMS):* Frameworks for real-time reporting and freezing of defrauded financial assets.

### Global Standards
1. **Financial Action Task Force (FATF):**
   - *Updated Guidance for a Risk-Based Approach to Virtual Assets and VASPs (Recommendation 16 / Travel Rule):* Defines standards for counterparty VASP identification and cross-border attribution.
