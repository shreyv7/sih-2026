## 1. Executive Summary & The Glass-Box Mandate

- **The Core Analytical Role:** Role 6 serves as the **Decision and Synthesis Layer** of the SIH26183 system. It receives the transaction paths from **Role 2** (Tracing), the entity attributes and exchange labels from **Role 3** (Attribution), the structural patterns from **Role 4** (Typologies), and normalized ledger data from **Role 5** (Ingestion). It synthesizes these disparate inputs into a transparent, mathematically grounded, and legally admissible **Risk Assessment**.
- **The "Glass-Box" vs. "Black-Box" Mandate:** Commercial AML platforms often generate proprietary, unexplainable risk scores (e.g., *"Risk Score: 8.9 / 10"*). Under Indian evidentiary law (**Section 63 of Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023** / former Section 65B IEA), opaque machine-learning outputs lacking deterministic mathematical explanations are inadmissible in criminal proceedings. Role 6 implements a **Glass-Box Scoring Architecture**: every risk score is an additive, auditable aggregation of verifiable on-chain facts.
- **The "Rules vs. ML" Engineering Reality:** Academic literature and commercial benchmarks confirm that **over 85% of frontline financial crime detection on blockchain must be deterministic and rule-based**. Rules provide instant execution, zero training latency, and 100% legal explainability. Machine learning is integrated surgically:
  1. *Unsupervised Anomaly Detection (Isolation Forest):* Used as an auxiliary signal to detect novel, uncatalogued topological outliers.
  2. *Supervised Tabular Classifiers (XGBoost / Random Forest):* Benchmarked against standardized datasets (e.g., Elliptic) for baseline validation, but secondary to deterministic heuristics.
  3. *Graph Neural Networks (GNNs):* Evaluated theoretically, but explicitly excluded from the hackathon prototype due to severe label scarcity, high inference latency, and excessive infrastructure complexity.
- **Anti-Overclaiming Principle:** The system evaluates **transaction flows**, not criminal guilt. Role 6 never outputs *"This individual is guilty of fraud."* It outputs: *"High-Risk Transaction Flow ($88/100$) characterized by multi-victim fan-in, rapid burner forwarding, and terminal sweep into an unlabelled Binance custodial deposit address."*

## 4. The Additive Explainable Risk Scoring Engine

To completely eliminate black-box unpredictability, Role 6 formalizes an **Additive, Point-Based Risk Scoring Formula**. Every point added or deducted is tied directly to a visible on-chain event:

### 4.1 The Formal Risk Equation
$$R_{\text{total}}(u) = \min\left(100, \; \max\left(0, \; \sum_{i=1}^{M} w_i \cdot \phi_i(u) + \alpha \cdot A_{\text{anomaly}}(u) - \sum_{j=1}^{P} m_j \cdot \psi_j(u)\right)\right)$$

Where:
- $\phi_i(u) \in \{0, 1\}$: Binary indicators for deterministic rule triggers ($R_1 \dots R_5$).
- $w_i$: Positive weights assigned to structural risk signals.
- $A_{\text{anomaly}}(u) \in [0, 1]$: Normalized outlier score from the unsupervised Isolation Forest.
- $\alpha = 15$: Maximum point contribution permitted from machine learning models.
- $\psi_j(u) \in \{0, 1\}$: Mitigating counter-evidence flags.
- $m_j$: Deductive weights for legitimate operational look-alikes.

### 5.1 Unsupervised Anomaly Detection (Isolation Forest)
- **Why It Fits:** In real-world cyber investigations, 99% of wallets are unlabelled. Supervised models fail when trained on small, biased datasets. Isolation Forest isolates anomalies by randomly selecting a feature and splitting the value. Outliers (laundering hubs) require significantly fewer splits to isolate than normal user wallets.
- **Mathematical Anomaly Score:**
  $$s(\mathbf{x}, n) = 2^{-\frac{\mathbb{E}(h(\mathbf{x}))}{c(n)}}$$
  where $h(\mathbf{x})$ is the path length to isolate sample $\mathbf{x}$, and $c(n)$ is the average path length of an unsuccessful search in a Binary Search Tree of $n$ samples.
- **Operational Integration:** If $s(\mathbf{x}, n) \ge 0.65$, the engine contributes $+10$ points to the additive risk score under the label `"Unsupervised Topological Anomaly Detected"`.

### 5.2 Supervised Learning (XGBoost Benchmark)
- **The Elliptic Benchmark:** The Elliptic dataset maps 203,769 Bitcoin transactions (4,545 illicit, 42,019 licit, 157,205 unknown) across 49 time steps. Random Forest and XGBoost achieve $>96\%$ precision on this offline benchmark.
- **Why Supervised Models Cannot Be the Primary Trigger in India:**
  1. *Concept Drift:* Laundering scripts evolve weekly. Models trained on historical data fail on novel scam mechanics.
  2. *Chain Mismatch:* The Elliptic dataset is Bitcoin UTXO-based. Applying it directly to TRON TRC-20 or EVM token transfers produces severe domain adaptation failure.
  3. *Inadmissibility:* A decision tree ensemble with 500 estimators cannot be explained line-by-line to a judicial magistrate.

### 5.3 Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) — Why They Are Out of Scope for MVP
- Academic papers frequently cite Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) and Graph Attention Networks (GATs) for node classification.
- **Technical Reasons for Exclusion:**
  - *Inference Latency:* Generating sub-graph embeddings on dynamic, real-time transaction graphs requires 15–45 seconds per query, violating our $\le 10\text{-second}$ triage SLA.
  - *Dependency Overhead:* Requires PyTorch Geometric, CUDA GPU hardware acceleration, and complex graph serialization pipelines unsuited for lightweight deployment at police stations.
  - *Explainability Deficit:* GNN embeddings are non-linear transformations across latent spaces, making them impossible to justify under Indian evidence standards.

## 10. Strategic Evaluator Q&A & Defensibility Guide

### Q1: Why not build a pure Deep Learning / GNN model to predict fraud?
**Answer:** A pure deep learning model is an uninterpretable black box. In an Indian court of law under **Section 63 BSA, 2023**, evidence must be deterministically verifiable. A public prosecutor cannot defend a conviction by stating *"the neural network generated a 0.94 probability."* Our system outputs an additive, transparent score where every point is linked to an immutable on-chain transaction hash and timestamp, satisfying the strictest judicial standards of evidence.

### Q2: How does your system distinguish between a scammer laundering money and an ordinary user depositing funds into Binance?
**Answer:** Through our **Upstream Lineage and Co-Occurrence Check**. An ordinary user depositing savings has a normal transaction history (salary inflows, personal transfers) with long holding times (days or weeks). The scam flow exhibits a distinctive structural fingerprint: **Multi-victim fan-in $\to$ rapid relay through burner wallets ($<15$ mins) $\to$ immediate deposit into an exchange.** Ordinary users do not move funds through burner wallets within minutes of receiving multiple third-party deposits.

### Q3: What is the exact role of Machine Learning in your architecture?
**Answer:** Machine learning acts as an **auxiliary validator, not the primary judge**. We use an unsupervised **Isolation Forest** to compute an anomaly score on the 12-dimensional feature vector, alerting investigators to novel money-movement patterns that may bypass predefined rules. It is capped at a maximum contribution of 15 points to ensure it cannot generate false critical alerts on its own.

### Q4: How do you handle false positives caused by cryptocurrency payment processors like BitPay?
**Answer:** Our engine checks all destination and intermediate addresses against a verified registry of commercial merchant contracts and payment gateways. If an address is verified as a payment processor, the engine applies an automatic deduction of 40 points, preventing legitimate merchants from being falsely flagged.
